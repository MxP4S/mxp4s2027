# Call for Papers

The Second Workshop on Mixed- and Adaptive-Precision Numerics for Scientific Computing (MxP4S 2027) invites original research papers and experience reports on the theory, algorithms, software, hardware, and scientific applications of mixed- and adaptive-precision computing.

MxP4S 2027 will be a **half-day, in-person workshop** held in conjunction with the [41st IEEE International Parallel & Distributed Processing Symposium (IPDPS 2027)](https://www.ipdps.org/) at the Hyatt Regency Bellevue in the Seattle metropolitan area. The workshop is scheduled for **Wednesday morning, June 2, 2027**. The program will feature **six selected-paper presentations**, invited contributions, and opportunities for discussion across the numerical methods, scientific computing, software, and hardware communities.

## Submission and review process

- Submissions will be handled through the IPDPS Linklings system. The submission link will be posted when it becomes available.
- Each paper will receive at least three peer reviews.
- Accepted papers will appear in the IPDPS workshop proceedings, which are submitted for inclusion in the IEEE Xplore Digital Library.
- At least one author of each accepted paper must register for IPDPS 2027 and present the work in person.
- To maintain an independent review process, no more than one accepted paper may include a workshop organizer as a co-author.
- The required paper format, page limit, and detailed submission instructions will be posted after they are confirmed with IPDPS.

Authors may indicate whether they wish to be considered for **a paper presentation only**, **either a paper presentation or a poster**, or **a poster only**. Poster selections will consider reviewer assessments, author preferences, and the number of poster slots allocated to the workshop. Reviewers may also nominate outstanding submissions for a Best Paper Award.

## Scope

MxP4S 2027 aims to foster cross-disciplinary communication among computational scientists, numerical analysts, numerical software developers, computer scientists, hardware architects, and HPC facility professionals. The workshop welcomes both theoretical contributions and practical experience with mixed- and adaptive-precision numerical methods for scientific computing.

Submissions should discuss both the benefits and limitations of the proposed approaches. A central consideration is whether the proposed strategy is applicable to one or more scientific domains or computational motifs.

The workshop also welcomes research addressing memory- and communication-bound applications. For these workloads, the benefits of reduced precision may come not only from faster arithmetic, but also from reduced memory traffic, storage requirements, and communication volume. Submissions should examine the associated accuracy, stability, reliability, and performance tradeoffs.

Research involving artificial intelligence is welcome when it advances mixed-precision methods for scientific computing. Work that only applies established mixed-precision techniques to conventional AI model training or inference is outside the workshop's scope.

## Topics of interest

Specific topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

- Theory and applications of iterative refinement using multiple numerical precisions
- Evaluation and application of Ozaki schemes and related techniques that approximate high-precision numerical operations using low-precision hardware
- Methods that enable matrix engines, tensor cores, and other low-precision accelerators to support scientific applications
- Mixed-precision algorithms for computational motifs beyond matrix-matrix multiplication and dense linear systems
- Algorithms and software that use lower-precision formats to accelerate memory-bandwidth-limited applications
- Mixed-precision methods for communication-bound and distributed scientific applications
- A-priori and a-posteriori error estimators for determining whether reduced precision is suitable for a workload
- Tools for analyzing, selecting, and tuning numerical precision
- Accuracy, stability, reproducibility, and reliability of mixed-precision scientific computations
- Benchmarking the mixed-precision performance of HPC systems for scientific computing
- Performance-portable mixed-precision numerical libraries and application frameworks
- Practical experiences applying mixed and adaptive precision to scientific applications
- Innovative hardware designs that improve the performance or efficiency of scientific simulations
- Hardware-software codesign for mixed-precision scientific computing
